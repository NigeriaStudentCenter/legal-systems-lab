**Course:** Responsible AI for Nigerian Legal Practice  
**Provider:** British School of Outdoor Education (BSOE)  
**Use:** Facilitator planning, fictional training delivery and proposed assessment moderation.  
**Regulatory status:** No NBA-ICLE provider/course accreditation has been confirmed and no NBA CPD credits are awarded.  
**Review sequence:** The course, animations, simulations, capstone and tutor must undergo **one consolidated expert legal, instructional and accessibility review at the end of production**. This is a development teaching pack, not approved Nigerian legal advice.

## 1. Instructional design and delivery model
**Audience:** Nigerian legal professionals with varied levels of AI familiarity.  
**Overall learning outcome:** Participants can design, conduct and evaluate responsible AI-assisted legal workflows while preserving source verification, confidentiality, professional judgment and documented human approval.  
**Course footprint:** 5 modules × 4 structured lessons = 20 lessons; five 3-decision simulations; five 5-stage case investigations; five document-based fictional case files; one integrated six-part capstone. Five video productions were initiated separately; confirm final video IDs before using them.  
**Delivery modes:** Asynchronous public pilot or facilitated live sessions. Browser-local exercise completion must not be equated with verified attendance or faculty-assessed competence.  
**Suggested facilitated structure:** Five 120-minute workshops plus independent lessons/workbooks and a 3–5-hour capstone. These are proposed educational timings, **not** regulator-approved CPD hours.  
**Prerequisites:** Ability to navigate a browser, read legal material and distinguish fictional from real matters. No real client information is required.

### Instructor safeguards, in every workshop
1. Announce **FICTIONAL TRAINING EXERCISE** before any sample rule, invented citation, client or document.
2. Do not represent hypothetical cases, tribunal names, legislative dates or snippets as genuine Nigerian legal authorities.
3. Never ask participants to submit identifiable clients, privileged texts or real active files to a public or unapproved AI tool.
4. Require participants to distinguish verified primary legal materials, fictional sources and AI-generated suggestions.
5. Keep the AI tutor in a coaching role. AI output is not faculty grading, attendance verification or approval evidence.
6. Provide text alternatives to animations and optional low-motion activities. Use captions, accessible labels and reasonable offline export alternatives.
7. Mark actual summative assessment only through approved human faculty governance; keep the public practice rubric formative.

## 3. Five instructor-led workshop schedules
Each workshop is approximately 120 minutes. Adjust to participant needs and keep a record of any changes.

### Workshop 1 — AI foundations and professional judgment
**Four lessons:** Model-generated answers; hallucinations and omissions; human professional accountability; safe prompt design and audit trails.  
**Proposed timing:** 0–10 diagnostic: “What does a fluent AI legal answer prove?”; 10–30 explain how model-generated text differs from an authority; 30–50 facilitated invented citation demonstration; 50–75 pairs identify unsupported claims; 75–95 guided case investigation stages 1–3; 95–110 draft a verification-and-escalation note; 110–120 formative feedback and exit ticket.
**Facilitator prompts:** “What could prove the alleged holding exists?” “Who signs off on this output?” “Where must uncertainty remain visible?”  
**Evidence to collect for formative feedback:** Source-verification register, three identified uncertainties, two-paragraph supervisor note.  
**Typical misconceptions:** A plausible case title is an authentic citation; second-model agreement is a substitute for primary source confirmation; passing a quiz establishes verified practice competence.
**Debrief:** Require each group to remove the invented citation and justify why it cannot support legal advice.
**Animated media cue:** Video 1, only after final render is confirmed.

### Workshop 2 — Nigerian legal research and source verification
**Four lessons:** Problem formulation; primary legislation and amendments; citation and holding verification; defensible research memorandum.  
**Proposed timing:** 0–15 issue-mapping from fictional instructions; 15–35 source hierarchy, commencement and transitions; 35–55 compare invented Rule A and amending Instrument B; 55–80 groups create a version table; 80–100 guided investigation stages 3–5 and challenge questions; 100–115 draft qualified advice; 115–120 exit ticket.
**Facilitator prompts:** “What evidence would make a 14-day deadline operative?” “What exact passage supports the legal proposition?” “What if the document cannot be authenticated?”  
**Formative evidence:** Issue statement, source version table, conditional advice note and reviewer checklist.  
**Misconceptions:** An amendment applies immediately when published; a summary's date establishes its applicability; the fictional exercise is a statement of real Nigerian law.
**Animated media cue:** Video 2 when completed.

### Workshop 3 — Contract and litigation quality assurance
**Four lessons:** Approved instructions; clause cross-comparison; witness/pleading source discipline; release checks.  
**Proposed timing:** 0–15 review fictional supplier instructions; 15–35 identify 7-versus-30-day notice conflict and missing schedule; 35–55 contrast AI rewrite with source contract; 55–80 groups create exception registers; 80–100 role-play the client clarification call; 100–115 draft senior reviewer stop/release checklist; 115–120 one-minute reflection.
**Facilitator prompts:** “Which commercial term did the client actually authorise?” “What cross-reference is missing?” “Why cannot the AI invent witness facts?”  
**Formative evidence:** Defect matrix, clarification message, document release checklist.
**Misconceptions:** A stylistically cleaner contract is therefore correct; the model may select unprovided deal terms; formatting review substitutes for legal sign-off.
**Animated media cue:** Video 3 when completed.

### Workshop 4 — Ethics, privacy and governance
**Four lessons:** Confidentiality and privilege; Nigeria Data Protection Act principles; vendor due diligence; incident response.  
**Proposed timing:** 0–15 identify material classes without uploading real content; 15–35 discuss confidentiality and data-minimisation principles; 35–55 inspect the fictional vendor questionnaire; 55–80 write processor/security diligence questions; 80–100 stage a stop-and-escalate incident tabletop; 100–115 review safe fictional alternatives; 115–120 exit ticket.
**Facilitator prompts:** “What evidence is missing from the vendor?” “How does a real client remain identifiable after names are removed?” “Who needs to authorise the proposed use?”  
**Formative evidence:** Data-classification table, vendor checklist, escalation note, simple incident timeline.
**Misconceptions:** Marketing compliance labels prove legal processing conditions; anonymisation is the same as changing a name; any AI assistant has permission to process firm documents.
**Animated media cue:** Video 4 when completed.

### Workshop 5 — Responsible adoption and capstone preparation
**Four lessons:** Use-case selection; human-controlled workflows; evaluation and monitoring; board-ready recommendation.  
**Proposed timing:** 0–15 compare the fictional firm's high-risk and low-risk use cases; 15–35 design a risk register; 35–55 analyse the invented test outcomes (8/10 useful with one fabricated authority); 55–75 build role/approval workflow; 75–95 groups challenge one another's stop/go conditions; 95–110 explain six capstone components and rubric; 110–120 Q&A and independent-work plan.
**Facilitator prompts:** “Would a high average overcome a single fabricated authority?” “What is a measurable success criterion?” “Who may stop deployment?”  
**Formative evidence:** Risk register, accountability matrix, serious-error stop rule, board decision summary.
**Misconceptions:** Performance metrics must only measure speed; human review means an undefined person glances at the result; a vendor demo is equivalent to a controlled pilot.
**Animated media cue:** Video 5 when completed.

## 5. Case facilitation notes and Socratic tutor boundaries
Every case has five stages in `guided-investigations.html`. The protected Azure API receives only a validated case ID, stage number and the learner's explicitly typed question. The browser-local 25-stage workbook is not sent to the model.

**Sequence for each case:** (1) frame issue; (2) compare source evidence; (3) locate defects and risks; (4) formulate escalation/reviewer actions; (5) defend a cautious decision.

| Case | One indispensable insight | Expected learner practice | Common unacceptable response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invented appellate authority | Invented citation cannot support a proposition | Exclude authority, document checks, escalate | Invent or assert a replacement holding |
| Fictional amendment deadline | Publication does not necessarily establish commencement | Version map and conditional advice | Apply invented 14-day period as real law |
| Conflicting supply agreement | AI may silently make unauthorised commercial choices | Exception register and client instruction request | Adopt a model-invented notice period |
| Unapproved document upload | Client confidentiality requires controlled handling | Refuse transfer, assess vendor, escalate | Upload actual privileged material |
| Five-lawyer adoption pilot | Aggregate accuracy cannot excuse a serious fabricated citation | Controlled pilot, veto threshold, named reviewer | Automated unreviewed client advice |

**Tutor facilitation rule:** Use the tutor to ask one targeted question, clarify the fictional task, and give procedural scaffolding. Never let the model certify legal correctness, impersonate a court/source, submit real data, grade its own output or report CPD points. Tutors and instructors should be explicit about source uncertainty.

## 6. Integrated capstone — marking and moderation pack
**Scenario:** `capstone.html`, fictional Eko Meridian Legal Chambers. Evidence: partner email with unsupported 30% efficiency claim, incomplete vendor questionnaire, fictional evaluation record and unsafe proposed policy. Participants should provide a 900–1,400-word *suggested* integrated board proposal across six required sections.

| Marked dimension | Maximum | Strong evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Business case, scope and assumptions | 15 | Narrow reversible use case, clear exclusions and valid baseline |
| 2. Source verification and legal quality controls | 20 | Primary-source trace, exception management, accountable sign-off |
| 3. Confidentiality, privacy and vendor evaluation | 20 | Data classification, supplier diligence, escalation and approved use |
| 4. Governance, roles and incident management | 15 | Specific role owners, logs, escalation, stop/release gates |
| 5. Evaluation, monitoring and go/no-go | 15 | Credible test plan, high-impact failure veto, retesting |
| 6. Board recommendation and rollout | 15 | Justified decision, six-week plan, budget and residual risks |
| **Total** | **100** | **Evidence-led professional judgment** |

**Proposed internal benchmark pending course-director confirmation:** 70/100, plus at least 10/20 in dimensions 2 and 3. A learner proposing upload of actual client files or adopting an invented authority as genuine should be referred for human remediation regardless of overall numerical score. This proposal must be confirmed before it is adopted as an assessment policy.

### Scoring anchors
- **Excellent (80–100% of allocated points):** Accurate identification of all supplied facts and contradictions; explicit controls with owners and evidence; persuasive, conditional recommendation; no invented authority or confidentiality lapse.
- **Competent (60–79%):** Covers most material risks and produces an actionable workflow; minor gaps do not undermine the central decision.
- **Developing (40–59%):** Partial controls, unclear source evidence or reviewer responsibility; revision questions required.
- **Insufficient (0–39%):** No reliable evidence trail, unsupported conclusions, unapproved data transfer or unchecked automated legal advice.

### Sample marking annotation
**Section 2 — Source verification, proposed 16/20:** “The answer identifies authentic judgment search, pinpoint passages, amendment checks and a recorded reviewer. It should specify how the team documents a search that finds no authoritative judgment and who signs off on excluding that citation.” This is a **hypothetical teaching example**, not a learner grade.

### Moderation process (instructor-controlled)
1. Verify participant identity and session eligibility through the authorised Azure system, independently of browser exports.
2. Ensure marking is performed by a faculty member assigned to the teaching session.
3. Score each dimension with a short quotation or reference to the learner's submitted work and specific formative feedback.
4. Flag uncertain source claims or potential confidentiality breaches for manual review, not automatic AI adjudication.
5. Sample high/low/borderline results for second-marker moderation and document any adjustments or conflicts.
6. Give a clear, respectful revision route with targeted tasks for failed criteria.
7. Certificate decisions require the secure backend's independent attendance approval and assessment outcome; they do not arise from this formative capstone alone.
8. Until regulatory approval, certificates cannot represent NBA-ICLE-accredited credit.

## 7. Instructor opening and closing scripts
**Opening script:** “Welcome to the British School of Outdoor Education's LexFlow professional learning pilot. We are practising critical AI-assisted legal workflows using deliberately fictional Nigerian-style scenarios. No invented case or training rule is authority for a real matter. Please do not provide real client documents or identifying information. Our goal is to learn how to verify sources, protect confidential information and make defensible professional decisions.”

**Transition to investigation:** “Read the supplied invented documents. Record what is known, what the model inferred and what still requires proof. Do not rush to an answer: explain what a responsible lawyer would check next.”

**Feedback script:** “Your reasoning should show how you reached the conclusion, which evidence you verified and where uncertainty remains. A clear limitation is more useful than a confident unsupported legal claim.”

**Closing script:** “Export your personal workbook if needed. These exercises build professional skills but are not accredited CPD attendance records. Any formal BSOE assessment must be submitted through the authorised process, with qualified faculty review and appropriate course governance.”
