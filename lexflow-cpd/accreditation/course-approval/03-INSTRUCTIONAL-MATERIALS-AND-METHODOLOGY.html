Document III — Instructional Materials and Training Methodology

Methodology

BSOE proposes a blended, instructor-supported online professional programme, subject to NBA-ICLE approval of its actual mode and eligible learning time.

  1. Orientation and diagnostic: scope, confidentiality warning, fictional learning materials, initial self-assessment and instructor overview.
  2. Structured instruction: 20 guided lessons, each with concept teaching, explicit objectives, a fictional worked illustration, workbook question and an immediate knowledge check.
  3. Demonstration-led learning: five planned original narration-led animations (one currently rendered; four failed after the video generation credit quota was exhausted), plus existing interactive demonstrators and text-based equivalents.
  4. Problem-based case investigation: five fictitious evidence files; each contains conflicting, missing or unreliable AI/legal-practice claims requiring source tracing, issue spotting and written escalation.
  5. Socratic AI tutoring: server-owned lesson and case prompts; the Azure tutor teaches methods and asks reflective questions; it is not a source of Nigerian judicial authority, confidential client repository, grader or certificate issuer.
  6. Independent professional exercises: downloadable browser-local workbooks, five three-decision simulations and 25 guided investigation stages with learner evidence notes.
  7. Faculty-supported discussion and moderation: five proposed 120-minute group workshops, subject to faculty appointment and delivery-mode approval; instructor scripts and observation sheets are drafted.
  8. Integrated summative assessment: six-section written capstone based on an invented Nigerian law-firm adoption dilemma, marked by authorised faculty under a transparent criterion-based rubric.
  9. Feedback and reassessment: instructional feedback, moderated marking and learner appeal procedures to be finalised before real delivery.

Example learning sequence (fictional citation case)

A learner reviews an AI-generated invented judgment, builds a claim-to-authority matrix, checks what primary evidence would establish the actual holding, records why the source is unverified and writes a cautious note to supervising counsel. The learner then answers a knowledge check; optionally consults the Azure tutor for structured questions; and incorporates the same safeguards into the final capstone. The exercise never claims that the invented judicial decision exists.

Study delivery and inclusion

  • Self-paced text accessible independently of video; instructor-led seminars available as approved.
  • Captions and full transcript for every final video; keyboard operation, screen-reader navigation and mobile checks must be passed before live approval.
  • Meaningful human feedback and alternative written exercises for learners needing accessibility accommodations.
  • Online activities conducted without copying real client files or identifiable information into public AI tools.

Instructor and platform quality assurance

  • Each instructor receives the 20-lesson delivery handbook and assessment/feedback templates.
  • A qualified Nigerian legal subject specialist must verify Nigerian legal claims and control of invented law before delivery.
  • Engineering must verify teaching/assessment separation, identity access, confidentiality risk and incident reporting.
  • Final expert review, browser accessibility, security and multimedia acceptance are not yet complete.

Live demonstration offer

BSOE can arrange a limited fictional-account demonstration of enrolment, one lesson, a case investigation, AI tutor, assessment submission and faculty moderation. Demonstration credentials must be shared privately with the Institute and must not expose real participant information or production resources.
