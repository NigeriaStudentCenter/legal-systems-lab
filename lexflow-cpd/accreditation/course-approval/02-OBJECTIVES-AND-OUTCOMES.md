# Document II — Course Objectives and Expected Learning Outcomes

## Overall objective
Equip participating Nigerian legal practitioners to use generative AI as a **controlled assistive technology** while retaining independent legal judgment, authentic-source checking, strict confidentiality and documented professional responsibility.

## Measurable learning outcomes
By the end of the proposed course, a learner should be able to:

| Code | Expected demonstrable learning outcome | Evidence of achievement |
|---|---|---|
| LO1 | Explain how generative language models differ from legal authorities and identify at least three categories of hallucination/omission risk | Lesson checks; fictional AI-output defect log |
| LO2 | Frame a legal research question, choose authoritative source categories and verify legislation versions, commencement and judicial citations without relying on invented material | Research issue matrix; citation audit; qualified memorandum |
| LO3 | Detect AI-introduced unsupported facts, contract inconsistencies and missing drafting instructions and prepare a review/escalation record | Supply agreement conflict register; fact-check/release checklist |
| LO4 | Identify confidentiality, privilege, data protection and AI-vendor risks in a fictional law office workflow and propose proportionate safeguards | Data-classification and supplier-diligence exercise |
| LO5 | Design a lawful, accountable and human-controlled legal AI pilot with clear risk ownership, source verification and serious-error stop conditions | Risk register, governance flow, evaluation plan |
| LO6 | Prepare and defend a professional board recommendation that separates verified evidence, assumptions, uncertainties and the limits of automation | Six-section capstone, human faculty marking |

## Outcome and assessment alignment
- **Module 1:** LO1 and the accountability foundations of LO3/LO5.
- **Module 2:** LO2; critical source-verification domain.
- **Module 3:** LO3; documented professional draft-review controls.
- **Module 4:** LO4; critical confidentiality and privacy domain.
- **Module 5:** LO5 and LO6; integrated implementation proposal.

## Evaluation philosophy
Participants are evaluated on **traceable reasoning and cautious, verifiable professional decisions**, not on their ability to produce fluent AI-generated prose. A fabricated legal citation, unresolved confidential-data transfer or unreviewed automated legal advice is a material defect even if the rest of an answer is persuasive. AI tools may coach but must not independently determine grades or CPD credits.

## Evidence to demonstrate institutional effectiveness
- Authenticated enrolment and approved attendance evidence (not self-reported clicks).
- Entry and exit knowledge checks, anonymised learning analytics only where lawful and necessary.
- Faculty-marked capstone criterion scores and moderated feedback.
- Accessible participant feedback and documented corrective actions.
- Course completion, withdrawal and appeals outcomes, securely reported only when approved.

**Validation status:** These are proposed outcomes and criteria; expert faculty and the Institute must verify final suitability. No unverified claims of improved competence or accredited outcomes are made.
