# LexFlow CPD — Instructor-led animation production package (scripts only)

**Status: preproduction scripts, not rendered videos.** The existing interactive visual training remains on `animation.html`, and five independent decision simulations are available at `academy-simulations.html`. These scripts can be converted into original BSOE-owned animated sequences after voice, legal accuracy, accessibility and copyright checks.

**Format**: 16:9, 60–90 seconds each, captions in English, low-motion accessible mode, original diagrams/icons only, no real lawyer likenesses, no client data. Use a visibly labelled fictional scenario and never present invented judicial citations as genuine law.

## Demonstration 1 — Stop the invented authority (Module 1)
- **0–10 s:** A fictional associate types: “Find a leading judgment about the fictional disputed clause.” Overlay: “A plausible answer is not a verified source.”
- **10–25 s:** The AI responds with a training-only citation clearly marked **INVENTED FOR THIS EXERCISE**. Narration: “Models can invent citations, dates and holdings.”
- **25–45 s:** Instructor demonstrates a four-box verification board: case name, court/date, authentic report or judgment, pinpoint passage.
- **45–65 s:** Empty primary-source result; the citation is marked **DO NOT CITE**.
- **65–80 s:** Instructor explains how to document the gap and escalate uncertain research.
- **Activity:** Learner completes the first decision simulation; success is judged by verification choices, not typing speed.

## Demonstration 2 — Legislation changes over time (Module 2)
- **0–12 s:** Two fictional instruments shown side by side: Rule A = 30 days, proposed Amendment B = 14 days.
- **12–30 s:** Instructor highlights that an amendment's issuance date and commencement can differ.
- **30–50 s:** Animated timeline: made → published → commenced → possible transitional phase.
- **50–70 s:** Learner is asked to list original rule, amendment, commencement, scope, source link and reviewer.
- **70–85 s:** Summary: “Never tell a client a deadline on the basis of an AI summary alone.”
- **Activity:** Version-check table and second decision simulation.

## Demonstration 3 — Contract clause redlines (Module 3)
- **0–15 s:** Fictional supply agreement displayed with 7-day notice clause and 30-day definition.
- **15–30 s:** Instructor highlights a missing Schedule 3 reference and the model's unsupported assumed term.
- **30–55 s:** A side-by-side “contract text / AI suggestion / reviewer action” matrix is filled in.
- **55–80 s:** A stop-sign appears at “Client instruction required.” The instructor sends a clarification request rather than inventing a deal term.
- **Activity:** Cross-reference review and contract simulation.

## Demonstration 4 — Protect client confidentiality (Module 4)
- **0–15 s:** Fictional junior associate attempts to paste a real-looking case file. A safety interstitial says **STOP — Do not upload real files**.
- **15–35 s:** Instructor separates personal information, privileged material, confidential business details and genuinely fictional inputs.
- **35–55 s:** AI procurement checklist: purpose, provider terms, subprocessors, retention, access, transfer safeguards and deletion.
- **55–75 s:** Secure approval route is shown; no real document is uploaded.
- **Activity:** Privacy decision tree and incident simulation.

## Demonstration 5 — The responsible adoption board meeting (Module 5)
- **0–12 s:** A fictional five-lawyer office proposes immediate automated client advice.
- **12–30 s:** Instructor compares low-risk template assistance with unreviewed advice and filings.
- **30–55 s:** Interactive risk matrix rates verification burden, confidentiality impact, reversibility, human review and serious-error vetoes.
- **55–80 s:** Board votes to approve a limited fictional pilot with review gates, not a production rollout.
- **Activity:** Capstone implementation proposal and final simulation.

## Production quality gates
1. Fact-check references against current authoritative primary law before publishing.
2. Narration must not imply an invented authority or fictional procedure is real.
3. Export captions/transcript and provide an equivalent text explanation.
4. For accessibility, provide pause/replay and avoid rapid flashing/motion.
5. Store final original video files in a controlled BSOE media location; do not link temporary signed media URLs.
6. Retain editable storyboard and a documented faculty review/sign-off.
7. Track actual video completion only after a secure learning-analytics design has been approved. These scripts do not currently confer attendance or CPD credits.
